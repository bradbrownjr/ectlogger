## Built around how nets actually run

A net is not a form. It is one person with a microphone, a list that will not hold still, and a room full of stations waiting to be acknowledged. ECTLogger is shaped around that job.

<div class="feature-grid">
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Net Control logs, everyone else sees it</h3>
    <p>Check-ins, status changes, and chat land on every connected screen the moment they happen. Stations who check themselves in flash gold on the list so Net Control notices them while running the net by voice.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>More than one frequency, more than one desk</h3>
    <p>A net can carry several frequencies, modes, and talkgroups at once. Several net controls can work them simultaneously, each showing which frequency they are on, with every check-in landing in one shared log.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Stations who cannot get online still count</h3>
    <p>Most of the field is on the radio, not the web. Net Control logs them by voice, and a station with no account at all still gets a row, a location on the map, and a line in the report.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Fast entry when the pileup starts</h3>
    <p>Speed entry takes a string of callsigns as fast as they are called. Any row can be clicked and corrected in place afterward, without opening a dialog or losing your spot.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Where everybody is</h3>
    <p>Locations go in however the operator says them: a town and state, GPS coordinates, a Maidenhead grid square, UTM, or MGRS. They land on one map, which is what situational awareness looks like during severe weather.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Nobody has to remember to close it</h3>
    <p>A net can open its own lobby before the scheduled start so stations arrive early, and close itself after a quiet period so a net left open overnight is not next week's problem.</p>
  </div>
</div>

</section>

## The paperwork writes itself

The worst part of a net is not running it. It is the hour afterward, retyping what you already wrote down once.

<div class="feature-grid">
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>The log is done when the net is</h3>
    <p>Closing a net generates the complete log and emails it to the people who need it: the net's own staff, the schedule's subscribers, and the owner. Check-ins, chat, topic answers, poll results, and a traffic summary.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>ICS-309, one click</h3>
    <p>The Communications Log comes out as a real FEMA form, not an approximation, with traffic-handling rows when the net has traffic enabled. Message bodies never appear on it; only the metadata the form asks for.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Formal traffic, properly handled</h3>
    <p>File an ARRL radiogram, an ICS-213, or an RRI weather strip from inside the net, with live text normalization and word counting. Every hop is logged against the message, so the delivery status of anything filed during your net is visible from the net itself.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Reports worth sending upward</h3>
    <p>A closed net exports as a multi-page PDF with statistics, the check-in log, chat, and the map. A recurring schedule gets its own report with participation trends and leaderboards for check-ins, Net Control, Logger, and Relay.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>A net that ran off-app can still be logged</h3>
    <p>Import a check-in list from paper or another program, supply the real start and end times, and close it in the same step, so everyone who took part gets attendance credit.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="feature">
    <h3>Credit where it is due</h3>
    <p>Operators can see their own participation history: nets attended, time as Net Control or Logger, and where they rank on the schedules they work. Turnout is a number, not a feeling.</p>
  </div>
</div>

</section>

## From a phone at a checkpoint to a desk full of monitors

The interface is the same either way, but it does not pretend a phone and an EOC workstation are the same job. On a phone, controls are labeled and sized for a gloved hand. On a wide screen, the script, the announcements, and the map dock into the page, with panels you can resize and that remember what you set. On several screens, the check-in list, the chat, and the activity log each pop out into their own browser window, so a net control desk can spread out the way it wants to.

## Yours to run, or ours

app.ectlogger.us is open to anyone with a callsign, and costs nothing. If your group would rather hold its own data, ECTLogger is MIT-licensed and self-hosts on a small server: Python on the back, a static build on the front, SQLite by default with PostgreSQL and MySQL supported. The <a href="/docs/self-hosting/">self-hosting track</a> covers install, deployment behind a reverse proxy, email, logging, and hardening.

Either way the data is yours. There is no paid tier, nothing is sold, and the <a href="/docs/PRIVACY/">privacy policy</a> is short because there is not much to say.

</section>
