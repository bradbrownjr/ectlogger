# ECTLogger documentation

Two kinds of reader end up here, and they want opposite things. Somebody checking into their first net wants one path with no decisions in it. Somebody who has run a hundred nets wants a complete list they can scan mid-net without reading a word of narrative.

Trying to serve both in one document is what produced the guide this site replaced: 1,084 lines, no landmarks, and nothing anyone could find. So the two are separated. **Tutorials** have a single guaranteed path. **How-to pages** assume you know what you want. **Reference** is complete and says nothing twice. Every page says at the top which of those it is.

## The four paths

<div class="card-grid">
  <a class="path-card" href="/docs/operators/">
    <span class="card-eyebrow">Path 1</span>
    <span class="card-title">Operators</span>
    <span class="card-body">You check into nets. Account and profile, finding a net, checking in, status and rechecks, chat, your location on the map, filing traffic, and your own statistics.</span>
  </a>
  <a class="path-card" href="/docs/net-control/">
    <span class="card-eyebrow">Path 2</span>
    <span class="card-title">Net control</span>
    <span class="card-body">You are running the net, as Net Control, Logger, or Relay. The desk, logging check-ins, speed entry, several frequencies at once, moderation, traffic, and closing the net.</span>
  </a>
  <a class="path-card" href="/docs/net-managers/">
    <span class="card-eyebrow">Path 3</span>
    <span class="card-title">Net managers</span>
    <span class="card-body">You own a net or a schedule. Creating nets, recurrence, net staff and the rotation, scripts and announcements, lobby and auto-close, reports and exports.</span>
  </a>
  <a class="path-card" href="/docs/admins/">
    <span class="card-eyebrow">Path 4</span>
    <span class="card-title">Administrators</span>
    <span class="card-body">You hold the Admin role on an instance. Users and roles, custom check-in fields, shared frequencies, branding, the maintenance banner, and security.</span>
  </a>
</div>

The order is deliberate. It is the order people actually move through: you take part in somebody else's net before you run one, you run one before you own the schedule, and owning a schedule is not the same job as administering the instance it runs on.

Nothing stops you jumping straight to the path you need. The paths do not build on each other, and each page names what it assumes.

## A note on what is documented here

This site describes what is in production at [app.ectlogger.us](https://app.ectlogger.us) today. A feature still being built does not get a page until it ships, because a page describing something you cannot find is worse than no page at all.

If a page here disagrees with what you see on your screen, the page is wrong. Every page carries its owner and the date it was last reviewed at the bottom, and a link to correct it. Say something, or open an issue: it is one person maintaining this, and the site is only as current as the last person who noticed.
